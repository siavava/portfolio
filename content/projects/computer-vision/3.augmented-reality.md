Overlaying one image onto another in correct perspective — the classic
augmented-reality effect. A book cover in a video becomes a screen: find
where the plane sits in every frame, warp the overlay to match, and
composite.

**A plane maps to a plane by a homography.** Two views of the same flat
surface are related by a $3 \times 3$ matrix acting on homogeneous
coordinates,

$$
\mathbf{x}' \sim H\,\mathbf{x},
\qquad
H = \begin{bmatrix} h_1 & h_2 & h_3 \\ h_4 & h_5 & h_6 \\ h_7 & h_8 & h_9 \end{bmatrix},
$$

defined up to scale — eight degrees of freedom, so **four point
correspondences** pin it down. Each correspondence contributes two
linear constraints, and the direct linear transform stacks them into
the $2N \times 9$ system $A\,\mathbf{h} = 0$, solved by the smallest
singular vector of $A$. `computeH` builds $A$ row by row and reads off
the last row of $V$ from the SVD; `computeH_norm` first conditions each
point set (shift the centroid to the origin and scale so the mean
distance to it is $\sqrt{2}$), runs the DLT, then undoes the two
similarity transforms, which keeps the linear system well-behaved.

**Finding the correspondences.** `matchPics` detects FAST corners in
both grayscale images and describes each with a
[BRIEF][binary-robust]
binary descriptor, then keeps a match only when the nearest descriptor
beats the runner-up by a ratio test. Real matches are polluted with
outliers, so `computeH_ransac` estimates the homography with
[RANSAC][random-sample]: fit
candidate models to minimal random samples and keep the one with the
most inliers:

```algorithm
caption: $\textsc{Ransac-Homography}(M, N, \tau)$
input: putative matches $M$, iteration budget $N$, inlier threshold $\tau$
best $\gets$ empty set
repeat $N$ times
  sample 4 matches from $M$ at random; f\/it $H$ by the direct linear transform
  inliers $\gets$ matches in $M$ with reprojection error $\lVert \mathbf{x}' - H\mathbf{x} \rVert < \tau$
  if $\lvert$inliers$\rvert > \lvert$best$\rvert$ then best $\gets$ inliers
ref\/it $H$ on best by least squares
return $H$
```

The implementation samples $4$ correspondences per iteration over a
budget of $100$ iterations, counts a point as an inlier when its
reprojection error falls under $5$ pixels, and refits the homography on
the full inlier set at the end. A sample of $4$ points is all-inlier
with probability $w^4$ at inlier rate $w$, so a principled budget
follows from $N \ge \log(1 - p) / \log(1 - w^4)$ for a target confidence
$p$.

**Compositing.** `compositeH` warps the overlay and an all-ones mask by
$H$ with `warpPerspective`, then blends the warped template over the
scene wherever the mask lands — dropping a new cover onto the book in
`cv_desk.png`. Frames for the moving version come from `loadVid`, and an
extra-credit `panorama` script reuses the same match-and-warp pipeline to
stitch overlapping photos. The full write-up is available as a
[PDF report][computer-vision].
