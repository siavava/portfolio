Recovering the 3-D structure of a scene from several photographs taken
at slightly different viewpoints — [structure from
motion][sparse-3d]. The
input is only 2-D pixel correspondences across images; the geometry
recovers both where the cameras were and where the world points sit.

**Epipolar geometry constrains the matches.** Two views of the same
point are not independent. If a world point projects to $x$ in one image
and $x'$ in the other (homogeneous pixel coordinates), the pair obeys the
[epipolar constraint][epipolar-geometry]

$$
x'^\top F x = 0,
$$

where $F$ is the $3 \times 3$ fundamental matrix of rank 2. $F$ collapses
the correspondence search to a line: for each $x$, its match lies on the
epipolar line $Fx$ in the other image. Eight or more correspondences fix
$F$ up to scale — the eight-point algorithm, a linear least-squares solve
for the smallest singular vector of the constraint matrix. With known
intrinsics $K$, the essential matrix $E = K'^\top F K$ factors into a
relative rotation $R$ and translation $t$ by SVD.

**Triangulation recovers depth.** Once the two camera matrices $P, P'$
are known, each matched pair $(x, x')$ back-projects to two rays whose
intersection is the world point $X$.
[Triangulation][triangulation-computer]
under measurement noise means the rays rarely meet exactly; the direct
linear transform stacks the constraints $x \times P X = 0$ and
$x' \times P' X = 0$ and takes the least-squares $X$ as the smallest
singular vector.

$$

**Bundle adjustment refines everything jointly.** Pose estimation and
triangulation each accumulate error.
[Bundle adjustment][bundle-adjustment]
corrects both at once, minimizing the total reprojection error over all
cameras $P_i$ and points $X_j$:

$$
\min_{\{P_i\},\,\{X_j\}} \sum_{i,\,j} v_{ij}\,
\big\lVert x_{ij} - \pi(P_i, X_j) \big\rVert^2,
$$

where $\pi$ projects a world point through a camera, $x_{ij}$ is the
observed pixel, and $v_{ij}$ flags whether point $j$ is visible in view
$i$. It is a large sparse nonlinear least-squares problem, solved with
Levenberg-Marquardt.

$$
% caption: Matched keypoints across views fix the relative camera pose through the
% epipolar constraint; triangulation lifts each match to a 3-D point; bundle
% adjustment then minimizes reprojection error over all cameras and points
% at once.
