This pipeline builds classical edge and feature detection out of convolutions.
Given an image, it smooths, estimates gradients, and votes for the geometric
structures those gradients imply, lines and corners, all in Python over OpenCV.

Differentiation amplifies high-frequency noise, so
[Gaussian smoothing][gaussian-blur] convolves
the image with a Gaussian kernel first; without it, single-pixel intensity jumps
register as spurious edges and swamp the real ones. The
[Sobel operator][sobel-operator] then estimates the
intensity gradient with a pair of $3 \times 3$ kernels, one per axis, giving
$\nabla I = (I_x, I_y)$ at every pixel. Its magnitude $\lVert \nabla I \rVert$
measures edge strength and $\arctan(I_y / I_x)$ gives the edge orientation.
Thresholding the magnitude leaves thick edge bands, so **non-maximum
suppression** thins them: a pixel survives only when its magnitude exceeds both
neighbors along the gradient direction, collapsing each band to a one-pixel
ridge.

The [Hough line transform][hough-transform] detects lines even when their pixels
are broken or partly occluded. A line is written in
normal form,

$$
\rho = x \cos\theta + y \sin\theta,
$$

with $\rho$ the perpendicular distance from the origin and $\theta$ the angle of
that perpendicular. This parametrization is bounded and avoids the infinite slope
that $y = mx + c$ hits on vertical lines. A fixed edge point $(x, y)$ swept over
$\theta$ traces a sinusoid in $(\rho, \theta)$ space; collinear edge points share
one line, so their sinusoids all pass through the single $(\rho, \theta)$ that
names it.

Detection becomes counting: quantize $(\rho, \theta)$ into a grid (the
accumulator $A$), and for each edge point add one vote to every cell on its
sinusoid. Cells where many sinusoids cross collect
high counts, and each such peak is a detected line. Real edges are noisy, so the
votes smear across neighboring cells; peaks are recovered by thresholding and
then a second non-maximum suppression over $A$, so one line yields one detection
instead of a cluster.

```algorithm
caption: $\textsc{Hough-Lines}(E)$ — vote for lines in $(\rho, \theta)$ space
input: a set $E$ of edge points, an accumulator $A$ over quantized $(\rho, \theta)$
for each point $(x, y)$ in $E$ do
  for each $\theta$ in the quantized range do
    $\rho \gets x \cos\theta + y \sin\theta$
    $A[\rho, \theta] \gets A[\rho, \theta] + 1$
return the $(\rho, \theta)$ cells whose vote count exceeds a threshold
```

[Harris corner detection][harris-corner] finds points where intensity changes
sharply in every direction. Over a window it
forms the second-moment matrix of gradients

$$
M = \sum_{\text{window}} \begin{bmatrix} I_x^2 & I_x I_y \\ I_x I_y & I_y^2 \end{bmatrix},
$$

and scores each pixel by $R = \det M - k\,(\operatorname{tr} M)^2$. Two large
eigenvalues — variation along both axes — make $R$ large and mark a corner; a
single large eigenvalue signals an edge, and neither signals flat texture.
