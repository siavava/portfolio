Tracking **optical flow** — the apparent motion of the scene across a
video, whether the objects move or the camera does. The project builds
up three trackers from first principles: a translation-only
[Lucas-Kanade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas%E2%80%93Kanade_method)
solver (`LucasKanade`), a six-parameter affine version
(`LucasKanadeAffine`), and its
[inverse-compositional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas%E2%80%93Kanade_method#Inverse_compositional_algorithm)
counterpart (`InverseCompositionAffine`, the Matthews-Baker
formulation). All three sample the image and its gradients at subpixel
locations with a `RectBivariateSpline`, and iterate up to a hundred
times or until the parameter update drops below tolerance.

**One equation underneath.** Assume a pixel keeps its brightness as it
moves. Expanding $I(x + u, y + v, t + 1) = I(x, y, t)$ to first order
gives the brightness-constancy constraint

$$
I_x u + I_y v + I_t = 0,
$$

one equation in two unknowns per pixel — the aperture problem: motion
along an edge is invisible. Lucas-Kanade resolves it by assuming the
flow is constant over a small window $W$ and solving all of the window's
constraints at once, in least squares:

$$
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \sum I_x^2 & \sum I_x I_y \\ \sum I_x I_y & \sum I_y^2 \end{bmatrix}}_{A^\top A}
\begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix}
= -\begin{bmatrix} \sum I_x I_t \\ \sum I_y I_t \end{bmatrix}.
$$

The translation tracker reduces to a $2 \times 2$ solve per window, and
the matrix $A^\top A$ is exactly the Harris corner matrix, which is why
corners are the pixels worth tracking: the system is well-conditioned
precisely where the image has structure in two directions. The affine
version widens the warp to six parameters, so each pixel's Jacobian is
the $2 \times 6$ block $[x\ 0\ y\ 0\ 1\ 0;\ 0\ x\ 0\ y\ 0\ 1]$ and the
per-step update comes from the pseudo-inverse of the $6 \times 6$
Hessian.

```algorithm
caption: $\textsc{Lucas-Kanade}(I, J, p)$ — iterative flow for one window
input: frames $I, J$, window center $p$, initial flow $w \gets 0$
repeat
  warp $J$ by current flow $w$ around $p$
  $b \gets$ residuals $I - J_{warped}$ over the window
  solve $(A^\top A)\, \Delta = A^\top b$ for the update $\Delta$
  $w \gets w + \Delta$
until $\lVert \Delta \rVert$ is below tolerance
return $w$
```

**Inverting the roles.** The classical affine loop re-evaluates the
image gradient and rebuilds the Hessian on the warped frame every
iteration. The inverse-compositional trick (Matthews-Baker) swaps
template and image so the gradient, Jacobian, and Hessian depend only on
the template: `InverseCompositionAffine` computes them once before the
loop, and each iteration solves against the precomputed pseudo-inverse,
then folds the incremental warp in by composing with its inverse,
$W \gets W\,(\Delta W)^{-1}$. The iteration reaches the same fixed point
at a fraction of the cost. The write-up with derivations and results is
[here](https://github.com/lostflux/computer-vision/blob/main/psets/04/writeup/main.pdf).
